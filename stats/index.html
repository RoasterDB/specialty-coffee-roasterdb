Where specialty coffee comes from

Colombia alone accounts for 25.7% of listings that name an origin, and Colombia plus Ethiopia together for 44.4%. The top five origins cover 63.6% of the catalogue; 19 producing countries appear in total.

What specialty coffee tastes like

47.0% of all mapped descriptors fall in the SCA Fruity family, 25.4% in Nutty/Cocoa and 18.1% in Sweet. The single most common note is Chocolate, on 32.2% of mapped coffees, followed by Peach (20.9%) and Cherry (20.1%). A mapped coffee carries 2.55 descriptors on average.

Flavor profile by origin

Kenya is the fruitiest origin in the catalogue: 62.1% of its mapped descriptors are Fruity, against 25.6% for Brazil. Brazil leads the Nutty/Cocoa family at 51.2%.

How specialty coffee is processed

Among listings that state a method, 35.1% are Natural and 34.5% Washed. Experimental fermentation is no longer niche: Anaerobic lots are 13.0% of stated methods.

What specialty coffee costs

The median specialty coffee lists at $17.91 per 250 g (interquartile range $14.62 to $21.46). Panama is the most expensive origin at a median $60.29, 3.4 times the catalogue median; Brazil is the cheapest at $15.86. Lots naming the Gesha varietal carry a 1.83x premium: median $33.56 against $18.38 for other named varietals.

Method, reuse and citation

  • Source. The full RoasterDB snapshot of 2026-10-04: 12,680 product listings collected from the public online storefronts of 278 specialty roasters in 23 countries, each with its source URL and crawl timestamp. 6,358 listings form the verified tier (origin, sanitized price and SCA mapping all present).
  • Coverage is uneven by design. Storefronts do not all publish origin, process, roast level, varietal, elevation or price. Every figure above names its denominator, so a percentage is always a share of the listings that state that attribute, never of the whole catalogue. Blank fields are never guessed.
  • Refresh. The catalogue is re-crawled monthly; this page and its charts are regenerated after each refresh, so figures move. Cite the snapshot date.
  • Reuse. The figures and charts on this page are published under CC BY 4.0: use them in articles, slides and posts with a link to https://roasterdb.dataengineered.io/stats/. The machine-readable version is data.json. The underlying row-level dataset is a separate commercial product.
